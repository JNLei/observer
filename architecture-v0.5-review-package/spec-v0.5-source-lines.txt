0016: > Authority and change control
0017: > This document is the project-level source of truth for agreed architecture and scope. It separates CONFIRMED decisions from PROPOSED / NOT YET APPROVED designs and DEFERRED implementation choices. An implementation session MUST NOT silently promote a proposed or deferred item to a confirmed requirement. Resolve only the decisions that block the task being implemented; ask the project owner at that point. If an implementation conflicts with a confirmed decision, stop and propose an Architecture Decision Record (ADR) instead of making the change implicitly.

0019: ## 0. How to use this document in implementation sessions
0020: 
0021: 1. Read Sections 1–5 before starting architecture-affecting work. Read the relevant subsystem section and the acceptance tests in Section 13. For agent observability/evaluation, also read Sections 17–22. For performance or backend implementation, read Sections 23–25.
0022: 
0023: 1. Follow requirements marked CONFIRMED and with IDs REQ-* or decisions ADR-*.
0024: 
0025: 1. Treat items marked PROPOSED as design starting points, not settled external APIs or mandatory technologies.
0026: 
0027: 1. Treat DEFERRED choices as deliberately unresolved. When a specific implementation task truly needs the choice, offer a small number of options, trade-offs, and a recommended default; obtain the owner's decision before locking it in.
0028: 
0029: 1. Do not ask the owner to resolve every deferred item in advance. Do not invent decisions to avoid asking later.
0030: 
0031: 1. Add new architectural decisions to the decision register, with rationale and affected requirements. Keep the document versioned.
0032: 
0033: 1. Preserve API-only operation and authorization invariants even in a local prototype.
0034: 
0035: 1. Do not turn an illustrative code snippet, JSON object, technology example, benchmark number, or proposed service name into a hard requirement without an explicit decision.
0036: 
0037: Requirement language: MUST = required for architectural compliance; SHOULD = expected unless a documented, justified exception is approved; MAY = optional. Status takes precedence: a PROPOSED MUST describes the proposed design, not an approved binding requirement.
0038: 
0039: Decision statuses: CONFIRMED / LOCKED (approved architectural responsibility or invariant); PREFERRED (approved implementation direction, subject to the stated validation gate); PROPOSED (concrete representation or default awaiting validation); DEFERRED (hard choice not yet settled); SUPERSEDED (historical decision replaced explicitly); REFERENCE (external example, not a platform requirement). Locked does not mean impossible to revise: a conflicting change requires an explicit ADR. A required acceptance procedure does not imply its numeric threshold has been selected or that it has passed.

0041: ## 1. Product vision and scope
0042: 
0043: ### 1.1 Vision — CONFIRMED
0044: 
0045: Create a pure open-source, AI-agent-native observability platform. AI agents are first-class consumers of observability and operational data; the platform should help them investigate what happened, understand service and infrastructure context, test hypotheses, and provide prompt, accurate, evidence-backed insights. The platform must support extensive agent customization and company/project-specific context. It must also retain strong conventional observability operations and deployment/IaC support.
0046: 
0047: Concise position: Machine-first investigation over reliable, modular observability infrastructure; human interfaces are optional clients, not the backend's reason to exist.
0048: 
0049: ### 1.2 Success criteria — CONFIRMED direction; quantitative thresholds DEFERRED
0050: 
0051: - An organization can run the complete core backend without deploying the project's default UI.
0052: 
0053: - Organizations can build their own UI or integrate their own agent using versioned, documented APIs.
0054: 
0055: - Observability ingestion, storage, raw queries, and conventional alerting remain useful when all LLM/agent components are unavailable.
0056: 
0057: - Agents can retrieve telemetry and operational context through a coherent contract without being coupled to a single storage engine.
0058: 
0059: - Every high-level conclusion can be linked to the evidence used, with explicit uncertainty and completeness limits.
0060: 
0061: - The product supports self-hosted deployment and standard IaC/DevOps workflows.
0062: 
0063: - Traditional metrics/logs/traces performance is comparable with established products for representative target workloads; it is not required to outperform all competitors.
0064: 
0065: - All core OSS capabilities remain available without an externally hosted control plane.
0066: 
0067: ### 1.3 Explicit non-goals for the initial architecture — CONFIRMED direction
0068: 
0069: - Do not select a final metrics, logs, or traces backend prematurely.
0070: 
0071: - Do not require one physical database for all telemetry types.
0072: 
0073: - Do not require one particular LLM, agent framework, or UI. Owned backend language now has an approved Rust-first preference (Section 24); SDK clients and independent OSS components retain their own languages.
0074: 
0075: - Do not make the AI runtime responsible for ordinary telemetry ingestion or routine alert evaluation.
0076: 
0077: - Do not require a commercial or hosted service; possible future enterprise offerings are optional and should not compromise OSS independence.
0078: 
0079: - Do not treat architectural examples from OpenObserve, Grafana, VictoriaMetrics, Braintrust, and other projects as prescribed implementations or exclusively as competitors. They are reference designs.

0134: ## 3. Architecture boundaries
0135: 
0136: ### 3.1 Four logical planes — CONFIRMED working architecture
0137: 
0138: ```
0139:                    OPTIONAL CLIENTS / PRESENTATION PLANE
0140:      OSS UI       Company UI        CLI/SDK/Terraform     External agents
0141:           \              |               |                  /
0142:                   Versioned, authenticated APIs
0143:                                 |
0144:                  MANAGEMENT / SECURITY PLANE
0145:        local management API | tenant & config authority | policy
0146:                                 |
0147:                  INTELLIGENCE / INVESTIGATION PLANE
0148:      evidence gateway | context | optional agent workers | evaluation
0149:                                 |
0150:                    TELEMETRY DATA PLANE
0151:          ingest/route | metrics | logs | traces | raw query | alerts
0152:                                 ^
0153:                         collectors / sources
0154: ```
0155: 
0156: The boundaries are logical service responsibilities, not necessarily four containers or clusters. Services can be combined for local installation if the required APIs, security, and UI independence remain intact.
0157: 
0158: #### Plane 1: Telemetry Data Plane — CONFIRMED
0159: 
0160: Owns: receivers, write path, buffering/WAL if applicable, indexing, storage, retention, raw query execution, backend-specific execution, deterministic alert/SLO evaluation, metrics/logs/traces availability and health.
0161: 
0162: Does not own: autonomous reasoning, organization-specific diagnosis policy, prompt management logic, evaluation of agent conclusions, UI session state.
0163: 
0164: Always-on principle: telemetry ingest and routine monitoring must not depend on LLM, agent worker, or presentation uptime.
0165: 
0166: Performance principle: metrics, logs, and traces may use different internal engines, physical formats, indexing strategies and scaling methods.
0167: 
0168: #### Plane 2: Intelligence & Investigation Plane — CONFIRMED ownership; interface details PROPOSED
0169: 
0170: Owns: agent-facing Evidence Gateway, semantic retrieval/correlation, operational-context retrieval, investigation orchestration, hypothesis/evidence tracking, persisted investigation results, agent evaluations, optional built-in agent workers.
0171: 
0172: Does not own: raw telemetry databases or privileged backend credentials exposed to users/agents, identity authority, underlying deployment orchestration.
0173: 
0174: Deployability: Evidence Gateway must be usable without running the project's own agent. Built-in agent workers must be independently scalable and disable-able. The exact number of services is deferred.
0175: 
0176: Important distinction: context source configuration and authorization are management concerns; context facts and their retrieval, versions, evidence and freshness are intelligence/data concerns.
0177: 
0178: #### Plane 3: Management & Security Plane — CONFIRMED
0179: 
0180: Owns: management API, tenant and resource configuration, identity integration, service accounts, policy definitions, agent permissions and budgets, configuration lifecycle, data-source registrations and credentials, audit policy, declarative configuration APIs.
0181: 
0182: Security authority: an independently usable customer-hosted local management/policy authority must be available. A future remote management UI is optional and may be delegated bounded management responsibilities, but must not be necessary for core OSS operation.
0183: 
0184: Distributed enforcement: the shared policy model is enforced at every sensitive service boundary (API, data query gateway, tool gateway, action executor, ingestion interfaces), not merely by a front-end permission check.
0185: 
0186: #### Plane 4: Presentation Plane — CONFIRMED
0187: 
0188: Owns: optional default OSS UI, presentation, custom UI adapters, CLI interaction and customer/hosted web interfaces. A CLI or Terraform provider calls management APIs; it is not the source of authorization truth.
0189: 
0190: Does not own: business-critical state or privileged permission decisions. UI deployment, upgrades, outage, or deletion must not interrupt core backend operation.
0191: 
0192: ### 3.2 Trust boundary distinct from logical boundary — CONFIRMED
0193: 
0194: By default the customer-controlled trust boundary contains sensitive telemetry, company/project context, investigation artifacts, authorization enforcement, production credentials, and privileged agent execution. Separate deployment units inside that boundary are permitted.
0195: 
0196: A future hosted UI or management experience may communicate through explicitly authenticated and authorized APIs; it must not require a remote vendor service to approve every read or ingest. External model calls are data egress and need explicit administrator configuration, redaction, and permitted destination policy. A hosted UI alone cannot reach a private VPC endpoint without an approved connectivity design.
0197: 
0198: ### 3.3 Failure-boundary expectations — CONFIRMED direction
0199: 
0200: | Unavailable component | MUST continue (given healthy dependencies) | Degraded functions |
0201: | --- | --- | --- |
0202: | Default UI | Ingestion, raw queries, alerts, backend admin APIs, enabled agent jobs | Default web experience |
0203: | External hosted UI/control | Complete self-hosted OSS operation | Remote convenience features |
0204: | Agent workers or LLM | Telemetry ingest/query, ordinary alerts, management | New AI investigations |
0205: | Evidence Gateway | Raw data read/write and ordinary alerts | Semantic evidence and agent tool APIs |
0206: | Management API transient outage | Existing telemetry pipelines and valid locally authorized operations within explicit freshness policy | Configuration changes; certain new permissions |
0207: | Individual metrics/logs/traces backend | Unaffected signal backends | Queries requiring failed signal; report partiality |
0208: | Query compute exhaustion | Ingestion SHOULD remain protected through workload isolation | Some queries may be throttled |
0209: 
0210: Caveat: These are design targets. Credential expiry, revocation and policy-cache rules must be specified before production security claims are made.

0259: ## 5. Traditional observability performance — SEPARATE WORKSTREAM
0260: 
0261: Confirmed principle: storage/backends are not selected because of agent architecture alone. The telemetry foundation must stand on its own.
0262: 
0263: ### 5.1 Reference architectures (not mandatory choices)
0264: 
0265: - VictoriaMetrics: specialized time-series indexing and ingestion; vminsert, vmstorage, vmselect in cluster deployments.
0266: 
0267: - OpenObserve: mixed telemetry ingestion, WAL, Parquet/object-store oriented analytics and distributed query.
0268: 
0269: - Grafana ecosystem: Mimir (metrics), Loki (logs), Tempo (traces), Grafana presentation/application server; independently deployed data engines.
0270: 
0271: - Other useful references: ClickHouse/ClickStack, Jaeger, Prometheus, OpenTelemetry Collector.
0272: 
0273: No decision is made yet that VictoriaMetrics is universally faster than OpenObserve, or OpenObserve is universally best for logs/traces. Architecture and benchmark results are workload-dependent. Compare actual sustained throughput under simultaneous ingestion and query, not only vendor-reported isolated query latency.
0274: 
0275: ### 5.2 Benchmark requirements — CONFIRMED direction
0276: 
0277: Separate metrics, logs and traces tests before mixed-workload tests. Measure at least:
0278: 
0279: | Signal | Ingestion | Query/read | Efficiency/reliability |
0280: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
0281: | Metrics | samples/sec, active series, series creation/churn | latency by query type, concurrent query load | CPU/sample, memory, durability, lag, cardinality impact |
0282: | Logs | records/sec, bytes/sec, indexing cost | text search, structured filtering, grouping/patterns | storage footprint, compression, compaction, tail latency |
0283: | Traces | spans/sec, trace completeness | ID lookup, attribute search, critical path | storage, sampling effects, latency, ingest impact |
0284: | All | simultaneous ingestion + representative queries | P50/P95/P99 | recovery, resource isolation, cost, tenant isolation |
0285: 
0286: Benchmark acceptance target: Comparable to established engines on explicitly chosen representative scenarios. Quantitative SLOs, hardware, datasets, cost ceiling, test methodology and selected baselines are DEFERRED until benchmark planning. This is NOT a requirement to dominate every benchmark.
0287: 
0288: ### 5.3 Known trade-offs — CONFIRMED as acceptable
0289: 
0290: - Indexing work versus flexible arbitrary search: accepted; benchmark after implementation.
0291: 
0292: - Sampling, aggregation, finite retention versus perfect completeness: accepted; expose the resulting uncertainty.
0293: 
0294: - Agent flexibility versus security: not a trade-off permitting weakened authorization; enforce the shared policy model.
0295: 
0296: - Additional agent query demand versus ingest stability: must be mitigated by budgets, priorities and resource isolation.

0298: ## 6. Canonical data and evidence contract
0299: 
0300: **Status: CONFIRMED responsibilities and principles in v0.3 following the owner's explicit agreement with the Evidence Contract.** The approved responsibilities are Identity and Correlation, Evidence Query, and Evidence Result. The six approved principles are common identity, precise time semantics, evidence provenance, data completeness, consistent authorization, and backend independence. Detailed field definitions, JSON structures, exact operations, enum names and wire schemas below remain PROPOSED or DEFERRED and are not API-stable.
0301: 
0302: ### 6.1 Why it exists
0303: 
0304: - Different backends use PromQL/MetricsQL, SQL/log search and trace search APIs; they should not dictate agent reasoning contracts.
0305: 
0306: - Agents need bounded, relevant, provenance-rich evidence, not unbounded raw telemetry.
0307: 
0308: - Custom agents should call the same stable operations as the built-in agent.
0309: 
0310: - A finding must distinguish no match from unavailable, expired, sampled, truncated, or not authorized.
0311: 
0312: - The contract must preserve sufficient information to reproduce or audit queries.
0313: 
0314: ### 6.2 Separate three contracts — CONFIRMED responsibilities with draft representations
0315: 
0316: 1. Signal/Identity Normalization Contract: identity/time/correlation mapping from native OTLP/Prometheus/backends into the platform's canonical context.
0317: 
0318: 1. Evidence Query Contract: authorized query requests, budgets, filters, consistency and execution metadata.
0319: 
0320: 1. Evidence Result Contract: results, references, source provenance, completeness, query costs and structured machine-readable errors.
0321: 
0322: Do not force all logs, metric samples and spans into one identical physical record. The canonical format is an envelope around typed signal payloads and evidence references, not a replacement for OTLP or backend-native storage.
0323: 
0324: ## 7. Identity time and correlation contracts
0325: 
0326: **CONFIRMED principles; PROPOSED detailed representations.** Identity, timing, correlation and provenance responsibilities are approved. The field names, mandatory/optional constraints, alias rules and serialization details below remain draft. The metric-cardinality direction is confirmed by A5.
0327: 
0328: ### 7.1 Canonical identity
0329: 
0330: Required concepts (proposed): tenant_ref (authorization boundary), service_ref (durable logical identifier), environment_ref, source_ref, and optional deployment_ref, cluster_ref, incident_ref, agent_run_ref, trace_id, span_id.
0331: 
0332: Map OTel resource attributes such as service.name, service.namespace, and deployment.environment.name into these identities without discarding their original names. Do not assume service.name alone is globally unique, or that the OTel service.instance.id is a durable logical service ID. Maintain aliases and a reconciliation policy for renamed or duplicate services.
0333: 
0334: Metric cardinality direction (CONFIRMED by A5; exact label rules/limits DEFERRED): Do not insert unique trace, user, request, incident or agent-run IDs as normal metrics labels. Correlate by bounded service/environment labels, trace/log metadata, exemplars where supported, and evidence references.
0335: 
0336: Trace context: Preserve W3C trace context across service/agent/tool boundaries where instrumented. OTel trace IDs are 16-byte identifiers, span IDs 8-byte; keep exact canonical lowercase hex for cross-backend referencing. Do not infer common ancestry solely from similar timestamps.
0337: 
0338: ### 7.2 Time semantics
0339: 
0340: Canonical time ranges are in UTC, with explicit units and well-defined inclusivity (recommended [start, end)), but exact wire serialization is DEFERRED pending API implementation.
0341: 
0342: Distinguish:
0343: 
0344: - event_time: source event occurrence time; for traces the relevant span start/end or events.
0345: 
0346: - observed_time: when a collector first observed the record if available.
0347: 
0348: - ingested_time: when the backend accepted/persisted it if available.
0349: 
0350: - retrieved_at: when the Evidence Gateway performed the retrieval.
0351: 
0352: - effective_time_range: the time interval actually covered by returned data, which may differ from the request.
0353: 
0354: Preserve metric resolution, aggregation window, rollup/downsampling and counter semantics. Do not infer causality from coincident peaks alone; clock skew and delayed ingestion must be representable.
0355: 
0356: ### 7.3 Correlation
0357: 
0358: Correlation must support, where available:
0359: 
0360: - trace_id/span_id for trace-to-log and distributed-trace linkage;
0361: 
0362: - bounded logical service/environment/cluster identities for metrics-to-service joins;
0363: 
0364: - exemplars, if supported, for metric-point-to-trace links;
0365: 
0366: - deployment revision, release, Git commit, IaC change and incident IDs for operational context;
0367: 
0368: - agent run/session IDs for linking tool execution to evidence and evaluations.
0369: 
0370: All correlations should report how the link was established (exact_trace_id, exemplar, service_time_window, deployment_mapping, inferred) and whether it is authoritative or heuristic. Never silently imply an exact join where only a temporal or name-based association exists.

0372: ## 8. Evidence Query and Result Contract
0373: 
0374: **CONFIRMED responsibilities; PROPOSED detailed API design.** Authorized bounded retrieval, provenance, completeness and backend independence are approved. Tool groupings/names, field lists, result enums, numeric limits and example JSON below remain proposals or deferred choices.
0375: 
0376: ### 8.1 Query API model
0377: 
0378: Two levels, both with identical backend authorization semantics:
0379: 
0380: Raw access tools: query_metrics, search_logs, get_trace, search_traces, get_service_context, get_deployments (actual names DEFERRED). Provide expert access with validated filters/parameters and backend-specific escape hatches, subject to policy and cost limits.
0381: 
0382: Semantic tools: service_health, compare_before_after_change, find_new_error_patterns, find_representative_failed_traces, investigate_dependency, retrieve_incident_evidence (actual names DEFERRED). These may call multiple raw tools and must return underlying source references and aggregation logic.
0383: 
0384: Execution limits: Each query request must support a server-enforced tenant scope, time range, bounded result size, timeout/deadline and (as needed) rate/concurrency budgets. Concrete numeric defaults and quota algorithms are DEFERRED. LLM-supplied filters are not authority; the server derives tenant and resource scope from verified identity and policy.
0385: 
0386: ### 8.2 Proposed minimum Evidence Envelope fields
0387: 
0388: | Group | Candidate fields | Why |
0389: | --- | --- | --- |
0390: | Envelope | schema_version, evidence_id, kind, created_at | Evolution and durable addressing |
0391: | Authority | tenant_ref, subject_ref, policy_version or opaque decision reference | Audit without disclosing privileged internals |
0392: | Entity | service_ref, environment_ref, deployment_ref, trace_id (optional) | Cross-system correlation |
0393: | Time | requested and effective ranges, retrieved_at | Distinguish stale/partial data |
0394: | Query | operation, sanitized parameters, fingerprint, backend adapter ID | Reproducibility and troubleshooting |
0395: | Payload | typed metric/log/trace/context result or summary | Avoid one-size-fits-all raw schema |
0396: | Provenance | source system, reference/locator, query or transform lineage | Verify claims against source |
0397: | Completeness | coverage, status, limitations, sampling/retention/truncation metadata | Calibrate conclusions |
0398: | Diagnostics | execution duration, limits, warnings, continuation cursor where relevant | Efficient investigation and safe pagination |
0399: 
0400: Sensitive fields: authorization scopes and backend credentials must never be returned as secrets. Query fingerprints/logging must redact credentials and confidential input. Source references must be tenant-scoped and reauthorized when dereferenced.
0401: 
0402: ### 8.3 Completeness semantics
0403: 
0404: Proposed top-level result outcomes (exact enum DEFERRED):
0405: 
0406: - OK: query completed; result may contain matching data.
0407: 
0408: - NO_MATCH: query completed successfully and no matching records were found within verified coverage; not proof that the real-world event never happened.
0409: 
0410: - PARTIAL: some records/sources/time ranges missing, sampling/truncation/timeout or aggregation may materially limit inference.
0411: 
0412: - UNAVAILABLE: source or required query unavailable.
0413: 
0414: - DENIED: caller not authorized; response must not reveal unauthorized resource existence.
0415: 
0416: - ERROR: failure not safely categorizable as a successful negative result.
0417: 
0418: Coverage reasons may include sampled, retention_expired, not_collected, late_arrival, truncated, backend_timeout, adapter_limitation, source_unavailable, unknown, as available. The system must not invent a sampling rate or a completeness guarantee it cannot verify.
0419: 
0420: Required reasoning behavior: A consumer must be able to tell the difference between "no matching records in available, retained and accessible data" and "there was no failure." Unknown completeness stays unknown. An evidence result can be syntactically valid yet inappropriate to support a causal conclusion.

0465: ### 8.5 Evidence references and reproduction
0466: 
0467: Each source reference should identify an authorized method to retrieve or verify the evidence, without embedding raw sensitive data by default. Evidence may be immutable snapshots or reproducible queries; these have different guarantees:
0468: 
0469: - Snapshot: retained excerpt or aggregate tied to a content hash/version and collection time. Better reproducibility, additional storage/custody.
0470: 
0471: - Query reference: sanitized query and time window executed against a backend. Lower duplication, but results may change due to late data, retention, reindexing, and rollups.
0472: 
0473: Proposal: support both concepts and record which type was used; decide exact persistence policy, hashing, TTL and reference URI scheme when implementing evidence storage.
0474: 
0475: No agent output should claim "verified" solely because the source was queried; verification requires checking the claim-to-evidence relationship.

0477: ## 9. Authorization, security and privacy invariants
0478: 
0479: ### 9.1 One identity/authorization model — CONFIRMED
0480: 
0481: - Subject types: human users, service accounts, standalone agents, delegated agents. Agent executions have their own auditable subject identity.
0482: 
0483: - Permission concepts: subject, action, resource, tenant, environment and optional task conditions. Exact policy DSL/model DEFERRED.
0484: 
0485: - Delegated agent rights must be bounded by user rights ∩ agent rights ∩ task scope; autonomous agents use their own service identity and task scopes.
0486: 
0487: - Policy definitions are managed centrally in the customer-hosted management authority; enforcement is distributed to gateway and services.
0488: 
0489: - Backend query adapters must scope actual underlying queries, not only approve the generic name of the tool.
0490: 
0491: - Default deny where valid permissions cannot be established; policy caching, offline allowances and revocation windows require explicit definition before release.
0492: 
0493: - Never trust client-provided tenant IDs as authority; backend credentials are never delegated directly to untrusted agents or custom UIs.
0494: 
0495: - Destructive changes/remediation require explicit, independently enforced permissions and (when configured) approval gates.
0496: 
0497: ### 9.2 Trust-boundary egress — CONFIRMED principle, implementation DEFERRED
0498: 
0499: - The customer owns sensitive telemetry and company context in self-hosted mode.
0500: 
0501: - LLM prompts, retrieved logs, tool inputs/outputs, runbooks and incident history can all contain sensitive information.
0502: 
0503: - External model use must be explicitly configured; redaction/classification and destination policies must execute before egress.
0504: 
0505: - Local/on-prem model integration must remain possible; no exact inference server or vendor selected.
0506: 
0507: - Hosted presentation/management is optional; private endpoint access must be explicitly designed, not assumed from CORS.
0508: 
0509: - All sensitive retrieval and privileged tool operations should be audit logged, with retention/redaction policies.
0510: 
0511: ### 9.3 Prompt-injection boundary — CONFIRMED security invariant
0512: 
0513: Logs, incident tickets, runbooks, Git files, and tool outputs are untrusted data for an LLM. These contents must never grant permissions or override system/operator policies. The execution gateway must enforce capability rules independently of model reasoning.
0514: 
0515: ## 10. Agent-native system behavior
0516: 
0517: ### 10.1 Principle — CONFIRMED
0518: 
0519: An agent is a first-class platform client, not a hidden privileged component or a model hardcoded into the UI.
0520: 
0521: Selected investigation responsibilities; detailed state machine PROPOSED: trigger → resolve service/context → form hypotheses → query evidence → test alternatives → qualify missing evidence → produce findings with references → request human approval where appropriate → record feedback/outcome.
0522: 
0523: The platform should let users substitute their own agent framework/runtime without changing telemetry storage or permission semantics. MCP is an optional integration interface, not the platform's entire internal design.
0524: 
0525: ### 10.2 Agent state and evaluation — CONFIRMED direction; schemas DEFERRED
0526: 
0527: Preserve, subject to configured retention and permissions:
0528: 
0529: - investigation ID and task scope;
0530: 
0531: - model/agent/instruction/tool versions;
0532: 
0533: - relevant company-context versions;
0534: 
0535: - tool calls, bounded query details and result references;
0536: 
0537: - hypotheses and alternative explanations;
0538: 
0539: - evidence-to-claim relationships and known missing evidence;
0540: 
0541: - final report, user feedback, eventual confirmed outcome if available;
0542: 
0543: - cost/latency/query counts for self-observability and evaluation.
0544: 
0545: The platform should not equate fluent model output with correctness. Evidence provenance supports auditing, not automatic proof of root cause. Human feedback must not be blindly promoted into universally applicable rules.

0788: ## 17. Unified traditional and AI agent observability
0789: 
0790: ### 17.1 Approval and scope
0791: 
0792: **Status: CONFIRMED in v0.3.** The owner explicitly approved the unified observability findings and recommendations in the referenced conversation, then requested incorporation of decisions A1–A8. The records below express those approvals as implementation requirements. They extend the existing four-plane architecture; they do not introduce a fifth plane or replace earlier confirmed requirements.
0793: 
0794: The architecture combines a unified telemetry foundation with specialized AI observation and evaluation models and independently deployable evaluation execution. This corresponds to the prior research's Option C for data architecture and Option D for deployment. Separate logical records and service responsibilities do not mandate separate physical databases.
0795: 
0796: ### A1 Unified telemetry foundation
0797: 
0798: **CONFIRMED — REQ-019 / ADR-A1.** Traditional applications, infrastructure, customer-owned agents, and the platform's own agents MUST share a common telemetry and correlation foundation. OpenTelemetry/OTLP is the canonical tracing and telemetry interoperability foundation. GenAI semantic conventions and adapters for OpenInference or framework-specific instrumentation enrich that foundation without creating an incompatible trace system.
0799: 
0800: Preserve original trace IDs, span IDs, parent relationships, span links, timestamps, resource identity, and source provenance. Agents, model generations, tool calls, retrievals, and handoffs must be correlatable with the ordinary application and infrastructure operations that caused or supported them. Parallel and asynchronous workflows must not be reduced to a fictitious strictly nested execution tree.
0801: 
0802: Exact supported convention versions, normalization mappings, collector topology, and compatibility behavior remain DEFERRED. GenAI conventions are still marked Development in the official repository; implementation must select and document versions rather than treating the moving upstream branch as a frozen platform schema [R20].
0803: 
0804: Unified identity and telemetry semantics do not require a single wire path or physical ingestion pipeline. Existing Prometheus-compatible and backend-specific interfaces remain compatible with the earlier architecture where selected; trace correlation and shared authorization must remain consistent across them.
0805: 
0806: ### A2 First class agent observations and sessions
0807: 
0808: **CONFIRMED — REQ-020 / ADR-A2.** Agent observations and sessions are first-class logical concepts. An AI observation projection provides efficient AI-oriented retrieval over canonical execution telemetry; it MUST preserve links to the original execution records and MUST NOT become an unrelated trace tree with independently invented trace ancestry.
0809: 
0810: The conceptual structure is session → trace or agent run → observation or span. A session may span multiple runs and traces; run identity and trace identity need an explicit mapping rather than an assumption that they always mean the same thing. Observations may describe agent invocation, model generation, tool use, retrieval, or handoff. Normalized attributes may include agent/model/prompt versions, provider, operation, usage, latency, cost, service/environment, and authorized content references when available.
0811: 
0812: Only identity, provenance, timing, and correlation needs should determine the mandatory minimum. Missing optional fields must remain distinguishable from known values. The precise field set, identifier formats, projection update rules, and session completion policy are DEFERRED. Content capture is governed by A7.
0813: 
0814: ### A3 First class evaluation and improvement records
0815: 
0816: **CONFIRMED — REQ-021 / ADR-A3.** Evaluation results, human feedback and annotations, evaluator/rubric versions, datasets, and experiments are first-class logical records with their own lifecycle. They reference execution evidence rather than overwriting it.
0817: 
0818: Support evaluations targeting an observation/span, agent run/trace, session, or experiment result. Evaluation sources include deterministic code, LLM judges, human reviewers, and external systems. Multiple or conflicting judgments and later corrections must be representable with attribution and history. A thumbs-down event is feedback; it does not automatically establish a verified failure or root cause.
0819: 
0820: The capability direction includes asynchronous production evaluation, on-demand historical evaluation, session/task evaluation, authorized annotation, dataset curation, offline experiments, and regression comparison. Exact release sequencing, score types/ranges, evaluator interfaces, correction semantics, and dataset/experiment schemas remain DEFERRED. Langfuse's current concepts provide a reference for linking scores and experiments; its product-specific schemas are not adopted requirements [R21].
0821: 
0822: ### A4 Independent asynchronous evaluation execution
0823: 
0824: **CONFIRMED — REQ-022 / ADR-A4.** The Intelligence Plane contains an independently deployable Agent Evaluation Service and asynchronous workers, separate from the built-in Investigation Runtime. Evaluation workers can scale or be disabled without making production agent responses, telemetry ingestion, ordinary queries, or deterministic alerting depend on evaluation completion.
0825: 
0826: External agents and external evaluation systems can use the supported APIs without running the default investigation agent. Evaluation may use code, local/customer-hosted models, explicitly permitted external models, or human workflows. Tenant permissions, budgets, and trust-boundary rules apply in all cases.
0827: 
0828: Evaluators themselves must be observable: retain permitted execution status, errors, latency, usage/cost, evaluator/model/rubric versions, and links to evaluation targets and results. Evaluator execution telemetry and the quality judgment of the target are distinct logical records. An evaluator error is not a failing quality score for the target. Langfuse documents separate asynchronous worker execution and evaluator debugging traces as useful references [R22, R23].
0829: 
0830: Sections 19, 21 and 23 now establish delivery/recovery responsibilities. Queue technology, exact acknowledgement boundary, retry/backoff values, idempotency key format, scheduler, quotas and code-evaluator isolation mechanism remain DEFERRED.
0831: 
0832: ### A5 Separate operational and quality metrics
0833: 
0834: **CONFIRMED — REQ-023 / ADR-A5.** Operational metrics describe execution health and efficiency: latency, errors, tool reliability, token usage, resource consumption, and model cost. Quality/outcome metrics describe evaluated behavior: task success, diagnosis accuracy, groundedness, evaluation pass rates, human corrections, and regressions.
0835: 
0836: Canonical evaluation records are the source for evaluation-derived quality aggregates. Aggregates may be exported to the ordinary metrics engine, but MUST NOT replace individual evaluation records, evaluator attribution, evidence references, or version history. An agent's self-reported success must remain identifiable as such and cannot silently become independent ground truth.
0837: 
0838: Avoid unique run, session, trace, request, incident, and user IDs as ordinary metric labels. Use traces/logs, evidence references, and exemplars where supported for individual-execution correlation. Exact aggregate formulas, eligible populations, denominators, missing-score treatment, label allowlists, and cardinality limits are DEFERRED. Operational latency and task-quality scores must not be conflated even when a dashboard presents them together.
0839: 
0840: ### A6 Shared authorization and complete headless operation
0841: 
0842: **CONFIRMED — REQ-024 / ADR-A6.** Observations, protected content, evaluation targets/results, annotations, datasets, experiments, and evaluation jobs MUST use the existing authorization model for users, service accounts, standalone agents, and delegated agents. There is no separate weaker evaluation permission system.
0843: 
0844: Actual retrieval, target dereferencing, content access, job execution, and result publication must respect authorized tenant/resource/task scope. Agent delegation remains bounded by user rights ∩ agent rights ∩ task scope, with its own audit identity. Workers do not gain permission merely because they are internal services.
0845: 
0846: All enabled evaluation capabilities must be operable through documented APIs without the default UI, a vendor-hosted control plane, or the built-in investigation agent. Telemetry-only installations can omit evaluation workers. Customer-hosted operation and local authorization remain complete. Exact authorization actions, sharing policies, worker credential lifetimes, and revocation behavior remain DEFERRED under the earlier security invariants.
0847: 
0848: ### A7 Content capture privacy and provenance
0849: 
0850: **CONFIRMED — REQ-025 / ADR-A7.** Capturing prompts, outputs, tool arguments/results, retrieved documents, and other sensitive payloads is policy-controlled and optional. Basic operational observability must remain useful without full content capture. Do not require unavailable internal model reasoning; only deliberately emitted, permitted execution data can be observed.
0851: 
0852: Capture, processing, storage, retrieval, evaluator use, export, and external model egress must follow customer configuration, authorization, redaction/classification, and retention policies. Large or sensitive payloads may be held in an optional protected payload store and referenced from observations. A separate physical store is not mandatory. Access to a trace does not automatically imply access to every protected payload.
0853: 
0854: Preserve provenance and visibility limitations: source execution, transformations/redaction, permitted content references, relevant configuration/evaluator versions, and missing/sampled/expired/unavailable data must remain distinguishable. An evaluation that lacks required evidence must report that limitation rather than fabricate a judgment. Corrections must not rewrite the original execution history.
0855: 
0856: Precise default capture mode, opt-in granularity, field classifications, payload limits, encryption/key management, redaction implementation, retention durations, deletion propagation, and reproducibility policies remain DEFERRED. Metadata-first capture with explicit content opt-in is a PROPOSED default, not a silently selected deployment policy.
0857: 
0858: ### A8 Physical storage selection remains deferred
0859: 
0860: **CONFIRMED CONSTRAINT — REQ-026 / ADR-A8.** No physical storage engine, queue product, index layout, object store, or mandatory database count is selected by A1–A7. Canonical execution telemetry, observation projections, evaluation records, operational metrics, quality aggregates, and protected content are logical responsibilities and may share infrastructure or use specialized stores where justified.
0861: 
0862: Select physical technologies only after representative traditional and AI workload evaluation. Include deep traces, large optional payloads, late evaluations, analytical score queries, ingestion/query contention, recovery, retention, privacy, and operational cost. Langfuse's separation of transactional, analytical, queue, and blob workloads illustrates one implementation, not a required stack [R22]. ADR-007 and ADR-013 remain in force.

0904: ## 19. Canonical execution and evaluation lifecycle
0905: 
0906: ### 19.1 Approved D1–D6
0907: 
0908: **CONFIRMED conceptual principles.** The owner approved D1–D6 after the v0.3 handoff; the v0.4 blueprint incorporated them. These decisions replace v0.3's description of the whole data-model decision as merely next/proposed. They do not freeze a full session/job state machine.
0909: 
0910: | ID | Locked decision | Implementation boundary |
0911: | --- | --- | --- |
0912: | D1 | Preserve OTel trace_id/span_id; add explicit run and session identities | A run is not assumed to equal one trace; preserve source identity and normalized mapping |
0913: | D2 | Preserve parent-child relationships and cross-run/span links | Parallel agents, retries and asynchronous handoffs cannot be flattened into invented ancestry |
0914: | D3 | Finalized execution observations are immutable authoritative records | Derived indexes can be rebuilt; corrections/new evidence use attributed revisions or additional records, not silent history edits |
0915: | D4 | Evaluations and human feedback have separate records referencing targets | Evaluation may target span, run, session or experiment output; it may arrive later |
0916: | D5 | Corrections/supersession preserve judgment history | Keep evaluator/rubric/model version, attribution, target version, reason and prior result relationships |
0917: | D6 | Quality analytics derive from canonical evaluation records | Dashboards/aggregates are rebuildable and do not become the authority for individual outcomes |
0918: 
0919: Finalized immutability is a logical history guarantee; it does not prevent required redaction, deletion or retention expiry. Such removal must propagate to derived representations and references under the chosen policy. Never regenerate deliberately removed content during replay.
0920: 
0921: ### 19.2 Lifecycle responsibilities and proposed mechanisms
0922: 
0923: Sessions group multiple runs/traces; runs represent execution attempts; observations describe agent, generation, tool, retrieval or handoff steps. Dataset versions identify reproducible inputs and experiment records identify configurations and outcomes. Exact resource schemas and enum spellings remain DEFERRED.
0924: 
0925: Distinguish source execution, evaluator execution, quality judgment and human feedback. Self-reported success is an attributed claim. Missing evidence, evaluator failure, budget exhaustion, skipped work and target failure must remain distinguishable.
0926: 
0927: **Preferred mechanism, pending implementation validation:** durable asynchronous handoff, at-least-once work delivery with idempotent logical publication, versioned evaluation targets and explicit completion/checkpoint signals. Timeouts may produce provisional targets, never silently final complete ones. Late evidence may produce a new target/result revision; evaluator changes produce new judgments. Publication idempotency does not guarantee a remote judge is invoked or billed only once after ambiguous failure.
0928: 
0929: Implement a recoverable handoff/reconciliation path so persisting a target and scheduling work cannot silently lose the evaluation trigger. An outbox, log or scanner are possible mechanisms, not selected products. Completion and worker execution must not block source production responses or core ingest. Queued jobs must recheck current authority and evidence availability before sensitive execution/publication.
0930: 
0931: ### 19.3 Still-required implementation decisions
0932: 
0933: Before public API freeze, define event/target/job/attempt/result identity and deduplication scope; finalization/checkpoint semantics; open-session fallback; snapshot versus query references; late-data revision policy; durable acknowledgement boundary; retry/cancellation/replay rules; expiry and permission-revocation behavior; aggregate denominators and supersession handling. Choose exact states and timers through failure/recovery examples, including a missing completion signal and crash between target persistence and job enqueue.
0934: 
0935: Required validation: duplicate submissions and worker crashes do not create unexplained logical double counting; all attempts remain auditable; target revisions do not overwrite finalized execution history; expired or denied evidence yields an explicit limitation; cancelled work cannot publish unauthorized results. These are acceptance responsibilities, not claims of end-to-end exactly-once execution. OpenTelemetry resilience, Langfuse's async architecture and session guidance provide reference rationale [R22, R24, R25].

Canonical traces additionally feed asynchronous AI projections; authorized evaluation submissions/feedback have their own APIs and worker path. Evaluation does not sit in the telemetry critical path.
0999: 
1000: Independent source collectors may buffer data before platform admission under the source owner's capture policy. I5 governs data entering platform-controlled durable storage; deployments must also configure source-side privacy when unredacted collector disks would violate customer policy.
1001: 
1002: A durability contract must name the actual failure scope it survives (process, node, disk or replica failure), when acknowledgement occurs and when the source may release buffered data. A memory admission acknowledgement cannot silently be marketed as durable delivery. Different adapters may expose different guarantees if explicitly documented.
1003: 
1004: OTLP has full-success, partial-success and failure semantics. In particular, a populated partial-success response is not a generic retry signal for the entire batch. Implement protocol-specific retry behavior; report rejected counts/reasons without echoing sensitive payloads [R26]. Collector persistence has finite capacity, retry and disk-failure limits [R24].
1005: 
1006: Unselected: receiver/distribution choice, queue product, WAL design, partitions, replication/fsync boundary, batching, retry values, idempotency key format, normalization schema and SDK compatibility matrix. Each selected adapter must publish its actual capability and loss envelope before release.

1008: ## 22. Semantic evidence, context, agents and evaluation
1009: 
1010: ### 22.1 Evidence API and deterministic execution
1011: 
1012: **LOCKED responsibilities; exact endpoints and schemas DEFERRED.** Expose both authorized raw telemetry access and semantic evidence operations. The Evidence Service remains independently usable by customer agents without the built-in Investigation Runtime. REST/gRPC/SDK and optional MCP are transport choices; MCP is not the internal architecture.
1013: 
1014: Candidate capabilities retained from the blueprint are service discovery/context/health, bounded metric queries, log search/aggregation, trace lookup/search, agent-run retrieval, recent changes, time-window comparison and evidence correlation. Existing Section 8 candidates remain valid design examples. Neither list is a frozen endpoint inventory.
1015: 
1016: The agent chooses investigative intent; a deterministic planner/executor validates and runs bounded queries. Derive tenant/resource scope from verified identity. Enforce time/result/byte/cost/concurrency budgets, cancellation, deadlines and pagination. Validate backend-native escape hatches with the same controls. Return source references, transformation lineage, effective coverage, freshness and structured partial/unavailable errors. Reauthorize evidence dereferences; a saved handle is not a permanent access grant.
1017: 
1018: Keep observations/evidence, hypotheses and supported conclusions as distinct logical records. A semantic summary is derived evidence with cited inputs and transform version; it cannot manufacture a causal fact. Findings may be supported, contradicted, unresolved or limited by insufficient evidence. These are conceptual categories, not final enums. Do not require uncalibrated numerical LLM confidence scores.

1035: ### 22.3 Investigation runtime and state
1036: 
1037: The replaceable worker consumes the same evidence/context APIs as external agents. Separate agent compute from ingestion, query execution and evaluation compute; use tenant budgets and resource isolation so an investigation cannot exhaust critical write resources.
1038: 
1039: Preserve the approved lifecycle responsibilities in Section 10: trigger, establish scope/context, form alternatives, retrieve evidence, test claims, expose gaps, produce cited findings and collect feedback. Persist auditable task/run/attempt identity, relevant agent/model/tool/prompt/context versions, permitted tool/evidence references, checkpoints and costs under retention policy. Private internal model reasoning is not required telemetry.
1040: 
1041: Completion, cancellation and restart must be observable. A crash or retry cannot silently erase prior attempts or grant broader access. Exact orchestration, checkpoint store, retry policy and worker framework remain deferred. Proposed remedies must cross an independently authorized action boundary with configured approvals and outcome verification. Production write privileges are not implied by investigation access.

1043: ### 22.4 Evaluation service and quality architecture
1044: 
1045: Evaluation is a distinct asynchronous service with workers. It supports code, permitted model judges, human annotation and external scores. Targets include observation, run, session and experiment output; workflows include production sampling, historical reevaluation, datasets, offline experiments and regression comparison. Release sequencing is deferred.
1046: 
1047: Evaluation must record target/version, evaluator/rubric/model/config version, attempt status, permitted supporting evidence, result and correction/supersession lineage. Separate evaluator health/latency/cost from the target's quality. Apply shared authZ when scheduling, retrieving targets, invoking tools/models and publishing results. Isolate untrusted code evaluators; choose the mechanism before enabling them.
1048: 
1049: Prevent uncontrolled evaluation recursion: instrument the platform's workers, but make eligibility/exclusion and budgets explicit before enabling automatic evaluation of evaluator traces. Aggregates require declared populations, denominators, sampling and missing/failed/skipped treatment. Compare agent/evaluator versions deliberately; do not mix incompatible scoring definitions into a single unexplained success rate. Canonical results remain authoritative [R21–R23].

1051: ### 22.5 Deployable services and optional hybrid
1052: 
1053: Implementation responsibilities may be packaged as ingest-gateway, telemetry-query, platform-api, context-service, evidence-service, agent-worker, evaluation-worker and optional web-ui. Names are illustrative. They are independently deployable/scalable responsibilities; a local combined distribution is permitted. Async projection ownership is in the Telemetry Data Plane; Intelligence serves the projection through authorized APIs and owns evaluation semantics. No fifth plane is introduced.
1054: 
1055: Headless operation and split self-hosted UI are supported product modes. A complete local management/configuration/policy authority is required for self-hosted core operation. Hosted UI/control may be added later as an optional client or explicitly delegated manager. It cannot become an unapproved dependency for local ingest, routine alerts or administration.
1056: 
1057: Raw telemetry, protected payloads, service context, investigation state, evaluation records and privileged action execution stay within the customer trust boundary unless explicit policy permits otherwise. External model calls are egress and require configured destination/content rules. Local inference remains possible. Remote UI needs an approved network path, authentication and backend enforcement; CORS alone provides none of those.
1058: 
1059: | Failure | Required independence / limit |
1060: | --- | --- |
1061: | UI or optional hosted management unavailable | Local ingestion, queries, routine alerts, APIs and enabled workers continue |
1062: | Agent/evaluation worker or external LLM unavailable | Traditional ingest/query/alerts and management continue; job lag/failures visible |
1063: | Evidence/context service unavailable | Raw telemetry and deterministic alerts continue; investigations report missing inputs |
1064: | Local management unavailable | Existing operations may continue only with valid local credentials/policy within defined freshness; high-risk or unverifiable authority fails closed |
1065: | One signal store unavailable | Other healthy signal paths remain usable; evidence reports partiality; failed path uses declared buffering/admission policy |
1066: | Query/AI load saturated | Ingestion and routine alerts retain agreed protected resources; throttle/cancel expensive reads |
1067: 
1068: Exact offline credential windows, revocation behavior, network topology and recovery SLOs must be selected and tested. This matrix does not promise indefinite authorization caching or availability through failure of required durable storage.

1070: ## 23. Recovery architecture and benchmark suite — REC-01–REC-06
1071: 
1072: ### 23.1 Why recovery is a separate requirement
1073: 
1074: The owner reported that a company Vector collector accumulated more than 24 hours of telemetry during an internet outage, then OpenObserve caught up in roughly one hour using 16 threads. **This is user-reported operational experience, not an independently reproduced measurement, language benchmark or universal requirement.** Signal mix, volumes, hardware, thread meaning, configuration, continued live traffic and the exact completion boundary were not established.
1075: 
1076: Rust alone cannot explain that observation. Buffers, acknowledgement behavior, batching, compression, write durability, network/storage bandwidth and concurrent work all matter. The platform therefore requires explicit replay/recovery design and measurement in addition to steady-state throughput.
1077: 
1078: ### 23.2 Locked recovery responsibilities
1079: 
1080: | ID | Decision | Required behavior |
1081: | --- | --- | --- |
1082: | REC-01 | Durable replay and controlled backpressure | Configured source/platform queues retain retryable data within declared capacity/failure scope; restart/reconnect resumes; overflow has explicit block/retry/drop disposition and observable counters |
1083: | REC-02 | Fresh-versus-backlog scheduling | Support bounded scheduling classes and tenant fairness; protect fresh telemetry and ensure backlog progresses; logical lanes need not be physical queues |
1084: | REC-03 | Late and out-of-order arrival | Preserve original event time plus ingestion/visibility time; declare signal-specific acceptance windows; handle query/cache/alert/evaluation implications and expose incomplete replay coverage |
1085: | REC-04 | Adaptive bounded concurrency | Adjust admitted/in-flight work to backend latency, throttling/errors and resource pressure within configured bounds; bound memory/queues and avoid retry storms |
1086: | REC-05 | Explicit delivery states | Accepted, durable, queryable and processed are separate milestones; distinguish rejects, partial results, duplicates, drops and pending work; queue drain is not proof of end-to-end completion |
1087: | REC-06 | End-to-end recovery benchmarks | Measure source outage through durability, query visibility and optional projection/evaluation recovery under concurrent live traffic, queries and background work |
1088: 
1089: REC-01 is finite-capacity reliability, not unconditional zero loss. Publish overflow, retention, disk failure and retry limits. An explicitly chosen lossy profile must identify loss, and cannot silently satisfy a lossless benchmark. Vector's buffering model and OTel Collector resilience document finite buffering/failure boundaries [R24, R27].
1090: 
1091: REC-02 may reserve or weight capacity for both live traffic and backlog, with hard admission ceilings. Exact weights, minimum progress, ordering and fairness algorithm are deferred. New-over-old scheduling can create out-of-order samples; enable it only where the selected backend/window and downstream semantics support it. vmagent documents concurrent fresh/backlog paths and these limitations [R28].
1092: 
1093: REC-03 requires documented historical-age/future-skew/out-of-order acceptance per signal and tenant. Accepted late data must be queryable at original event time. Rejected/expired data needs an attributable disposition, not silently altered timestamps. Cache invalidation, recording rules, alert notifications and evaluation target revisions must have explicit policy; replay must not accidentally fire old production notifications as if they were current. Mimir's documentation shows that late data affects query caching and rules [R29].
1094: 
1095: REC-04 requires a measurable stable controller or justified equivalent bounded strategy, not a particular algorithm. Provide a fixed-concurrency mode for comparison and diagnostics; cap retries with jitter/backoff. Separate CPU-heavy parsing/compression from asynchronous I/O so runtime workers yield. Vector ARC is a design reference [R30]; Tokio fairness depends on bounded tasks and nonblocking work [R31].
1096: 
1097: ### 23.3 Delivery state and loss accounting
1098: 
1099: | Milestone | Meaning | Must not imply |
1100: | --- | --- | --- |
1101: | Accepted | Admission/validation passed for identified records | Disk persistence or complete query visibility |
1102: | Durable | Configured persistence/replication boundary reached | Survival of failure outside that declared scope |
1103: | Queryable | Authorized queries can retrieve committed records for the relevant signal/time | AI projections, compaction or evaluation finished |
1104: | Processed | Specified derived processing stage completed | Every optional downstream workflow completed |
1105: 
1106: These are logical milestones, not a fixed wire enum or requirement for per-record status RPCs. Define stage-specific watermarks/counters/probes that can substantiate the claims. Include partial batch acceptance/rejection and ambiguous acknowledgement failures. Keep source submission counts, logical identities, retry attempts and physical duplicates distinct. A duplicate attempt is not necessarily a second logical event; conflicting copies need a defined reconciliation policy.
1107: 
1108: Reconcile produced/eligible records against pending, queryable unique records and explicitly disposed records under the selected model. Report unexplained gaps separately. Physical downstream duplicates, unsupported deduplication and probabilistic checks must be disclosed. Counts alone cannot prove content correctness; use deterministic IDs/checksums and representative value/relationship checks where supported. Do not require a custom per-event ingest ID to become a normal metrics label.
1109: 
1110: ### 23.4 Recovery capacity model and reference scenario
1111: 
1112: For a constant live rate lambda, backlog B and sustainable total useful processing capacity mu at the same completion boundary, recovery time is approximately B / (mu - lambda), requiring mu > lambda. A 24-hour backlog cleared in one hour while the original live rate continues implies about 25 times the steady-state useful capacity (24 times backlog plus 1 times live). This is an illustrative fluid-queue calculation, not a measured product claim. Compression, bottleneck changes, compaction, multiple stages, retries and autoscaling may invalidate a single-rate approximation.
1113: 
1114: **Reference scenario REC-REF-24:** run a 24-hour source-to-backend disconnect, preserve source data durably, reconnect while live traffic continues, and evaluate a one-hour catch-up aspiration. Include a constrained profile described as 16 threads only after defining which workers/runtime threads and the actual CPU quota. Sixteen threads are not automatically sixteen cores or sixteen vCPUs. Use the user's experience as motivation; select actual release SLOs through the workload charter. Faster scaled/compressed simulations may support development, but do not replace a wall-clock endurance run for a production reliability claim.
1115: 
1116: ### 23.5 Required failure/recovery scenarios
1117: 
1118: - Short and prolonged network loss, timeout/acknowledgement ambiguity, throttling and reconnect storms; include REC-REF-24 as a measured reference or explicitly unrun case.
1119: - Collector and ingest process restart during buffering/replay; node/disk loss according to declared durability scope; disk full, queue full and retry expiry.
1120: - Slow/unavailable signal store, bandwidth/disk constraints, projection worker outage and delayed evaluation backlog.
1121: - Duplicate/reordered batches, late spans, incomplete traces, poisoned records, expired/denied payloads and cross-tenant replay attempts.
1122: - Replay with ongoing live traffic, representative raw/semantic queries, deterministic alerts, compaction and AI/evaluation activity. Isolated recovery tests alone are insufficient.
1123: - Noisy-tenant pressure, fresh-data priority and backlog progress; compare fixed and adaptive concurrency; test reduced capacity and overload, not only unconstrained scaling.
1124: 
1125: ### 23.6 Benchmark measurements and acceptance
1126: 
1127: Report offered, admitted, durable and queryable rates in both signal units and bytes; source backlog bytes/age; time to reconnect, first fresh visibility and full eligible backlog visibility; fresh-data P50/P95/P99 lag; projection lag and evaluation backlog separately; missing/rejected/dropped/duplicate/conflicting data; CPU, RSS/peak memory, allocations/GC where applicable, disk/network I/O, storage amplification and cost. Report alert freshness and query tail latency during recovery.
1128: 
1129: Measure the slowest relevant durable/queryable stage, not only intake API throughput. Report post-ingestion compaction/settling separately. Define recovery end from durable/queryable watermarks and integrity checks; source queue empty is insufficient.
1130: 
1131: Before a pass/fail run, declare workload, hardware/resource limits, engine/collector versions, topology, durability, replication, retention/lateness policy, concurrency and batching, active live rate, query mix, allowed loss/duplicates, freshness/backlog SLOs and cost ceiling. Pass requires correctness, no unexplained loss, bounded resources, tenant isolation and the agreed fresh/backlog completion targets for that profile. Until numbers are approved, outcomes are measurements, not a parity or release-pass claim.

1133: ## 24. Language strategy and performance validation
1134: 
1135: ### 24.1 Rust-first preference supersedes Go-first
1136: 
1137: **PREFERRED direction approved after the outage analysis:** use Rust for backend code the project owns: ingest admission/routing/processing, telemetry adapters/query gateway, evidence/context services, management APIs, authorization enforcement integration and AI observation projections. Prefer Rust if future owned storage/query components become justified. This preference does not decide to build a storage engine.
1138: 
1139: Use Python for pluggable investigation/model orchestration and evaluation workers. Use TypeScript for the optional default web UI. Client SDKs may use their ecosystem's languages. Existing OTel Collector distributions, VictoriaMetrics and other Go components remain independent dependencies; do not rewrite them for language uniformity. Go may be appropriate for custom Collector extensions, operators or providers when justified.
1140: 
1141: Rust is favored for explicit ownership/allocation control, predictable resource design under replay and analytical workloads, safe concurrency and access to Arrow/DataFusion. DataFusion is a Rust query-engine ecosystem reference, not the selected platform engine [R32]. Rust does not supply bounded queues, fairness or recovery automatically, and it can still allocate excessively, block runtime threads or run out of memory.
1142: 
1143: Go remains a credible alternative with mature infrastructure libraries and accessible concurrency. Its concurrent GC involves CPU/memory trade-offs; low memory limits and high allocation pressure can reduce progress [R33]. This does not establish that Go cannot meet the requirements. Compare measured useful work and tails under equal semantics; do not infer a language verdict from two different products or older isolated case studies. Rust complexity, build times, contributor onboarding and implementation quality are material costs.
1144: 
1145: ### 24.2 Required production-shaped Rust-versus-Go prototype
1146: 
1147: **Required before broad owned-backend commitment.** Implement the same representative vertical slice in both languages: real protocol decoding and validation, shared-authZ enforcement, capture/redaction, batching/compression, bounded async I/O, durable retry/replay path, identical signal-store adapter, query/evidence response serialization and asynchronous projection handoff. Use a real selected candidate store/queue where feasible; label substitutions. A no-op HTTP benchmark cannot settle this decision.
1148: 
1149: Match functional semantics, durability boundary, payloads, compression, batching, auth policy, storage/network topology, resource quotas and background workloads. Pin compiler/runtime/library versions and release-build options. Give both implementations competent idiomatic tuning; record optimization time. Where ecosystem libraries or engine integration force different designs, report the difference as a stack comparison rather than isolate it as language causality.
1150: 
1151: Run warm/cold and repeated trials with sustained load, bursts, deep/wide agent traces and large permitted payloads; include mixed ingestion/query/compaction/evaluation activity and the Section 23 recovery suite. Report distributions, run-to-run variability, errors and raw configuration/results. Record CPU quota versus actual OS/runtime threads, Go GOMAXPROCS/GC settings and Rust runtime/blocking-pool limits. Include the defined 16-thread reference profile plus a resource-scaled comparison; do not tune one variant by dropping validation or reducing durability.
1152: 
1153: The decision record must cover throughput, P95/P99 latency, steady/peak RSS, allocation/GC behavior, recovery to queryable state, fresh/backlog fairness, resource/cost efficiency, correctness and maintainability. Include development/debugging effort, contributor skill needs, compile/build friction and operational complexity.
1154: 
1155: **Acceptance:** both variants must satisfy the same correctness/security/recovery gates before performance comparisons count. Review against the predeclared workload/SLO/cost charter. Rust remains preferred if it meets those gates with acceptable delivery/maintenance cost and supports long-term requirements. A failure or material trade-off triggers a documented decision with the owner; it does not silently revert to Go. No numeric Rust-over-Go speedup is a required outcome.
1156: 
1157: ### 24.3 Four benchmark suites
1158: 
1159: | Suite | Workload | Required comparison / measures |
1160: | --- | --- | --- |
1161: | Traditional telemetry | Separate metrics, logs and traces, then concurrent reads/writes; series churn, search selectivity, retention/indexing | VictoriaMetrics/Mimir for matching metrics paths, OpenObserve/Loki for logs, OpenObserve/Tempo for traces where capability matches |
1162: | AI observability and evidence | Deep/multi-agent traces, large optional content, run/session lookup, semantic correlation/context, late scores and quality analytics | Projection correctness/lag, evidence provenance/completeness, bounded retrieval cost, evaluator throughput and reproducibility |

1164: | Reliability and recovery | Disconnect/restart/overflow/reorder/late data; backlog replay with live traffic | Section 23 end-to-end milestones, integrity, fresh/backlog fairness and resource/cost envelope |
1165: 
1166: Reference projects are models and guides. Grafana's UI/server is not itself a substitute for benchmarking the corresponding Mimir/Loki/Tempo engines. Select equivalent durability, replication, sampling, indexes, retention and query semantics. Publish relevant feature gaps rather than reporting a misleading aggregate “winner.” The goal remains comparable fitness on representative agreed workloads, not beating every mature system.
